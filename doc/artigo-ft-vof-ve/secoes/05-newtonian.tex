\subsubsection{Mass conservation}

The two methods drift in \emph{opposite directions}, and that is the first
result worth stating. VOF gains mass; front-tracking loses it. The magnitudes
differ by a factor of four, but the signs differing matters more: these are two
mechanisms, not one mechanism at two strengths. Mass gain in PLIC is a
reconstruction artefact; loss in front-tracking is Lagrangian advection drift.

Both methods reproduce the \textbf{ellipsoidal regime} the reference reports for
case~1: the height-to-equivalent-diameter ratio is below one in both, that is,
flattened along the direction of rise. Both reproduce the shape of the published
rise-velocity curve --- the velocity grows, peaks near $t = 0.9$, and decreases
toward a steady value.

The difference in centre of mass \textbf{grows monotonically}: 0.44\% at
$t=0.5$, 1.03\% at $t=1.5$, 1.94\% at $t=3$. It is not a constant offset but an
accumulated velocity difference, consistent with front-tracking rising 4.3\%
more slowly in the terminal regime.

\paragraph{The instrument was cleared, not assumed.}
The VOF centre of mass is computed from the marching-cubes surface, which
encloses about 2.3\% less volume than the field at this resolution; that could
bias the centroid. An independent probe computes the centroid directly from the
field and gives $1.490212$ against $1.4903$ from the surface --- agreement to
\num{1e-4}. The volume bias does not translate into a position bias.

\paragraph{Topological soundness.}
121 output frames per method, \emph{all} surfaces closed (zero orphan edges,
$\chi = 2$). In the front-tracking run the triangulation grew from 5120 to 5558
triangles over the rise, with 8824 surgery operations and a partition-of-unity
residual of \num{1.1e-14} --- refinement where the bubble deforms, which is the
intended behaviour.
