Front-tracking is \emph{cheaper} than VOF on the Newtonian case, by about 12\%.
That is worth stating because the opposite is often assumed: an explicit surface
sounds more expensive than a scalar field. It is not, here, because VOF pays for
the PLIC reconstruction and the split advection at every cell near the
interface, whereas front-tracking pays for advecting a few thousand vertices and
rebuilding one spatial index.

\paragraph{Contention is not negligible, and the number has to say which it is.}
The rates above were measured with a single run on the machine. Two runs on
separate cores slow each other by roughly 60\% at $250\,000$ cells, through
memory bandwidth rather than CPU. Any wall-clock figure in this paper therefore
states whether it was measured alone or alongside another run.

\subsection{Output cost}

Worth a paragraph because it dominated a first attempt at the reference
resolution. The field output is an unstructured grid with eight unshared corner
points per cell: $250\,000$ cells become two million points, about 300~MB per
frame, roughly 20 minutes to write. At 120 frames that is some 40 hours of
writing against 16 hours of computation. The extracted interface is a factor of
226 smaller and already carries the interpolated fields at its vertices, so the
field output is written at a coarse cadence and the interface at every frame.
